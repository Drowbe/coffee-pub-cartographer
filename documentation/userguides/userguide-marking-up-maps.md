# Marking Up a Map

**Audience:** anyone adding to a map, or correcting one that has a square wrong.

Notes, symbols, floor surfaces, correcting the map, and the grid. Everything here starts by
right-clicking a square.

## Write a note about a room

Right-click a square and choose **Add Note**. Type what you want to remember and save it.

A note holds a few sentences, and the line breaks you type are kept. It shows as a symbol on the
square, and the text appears when you hover it. Right-clicking a square that already has one offers
**Edit Note** instead.

**Who can do it:** any member of the party, on any map they can see.

## Place a map symbol

Right-click a square and choose **Placeables**. Symbols are grouped by kind -- doors, stairs,
hazards, structures, terrain, furnishings -- and named as an old-school map key names them.

A square holds one symbol. Placing a second replaces the first. **Remove Symbol** clears it.

**Who can do it:** any member of the party. On an official map you may add your own and take your own
back, but not remove what its author drew or what somebody else added.

## Give a room a floor

Right-click a square and choose **Floor Type**, then a surface. There are three plain shades and
seven materials: brick, cobblestone, dirt, grass, rock, tile and wood.

One click surfaces the **whole area**, stopping at the walls and doorways the map already knows
about, so a room takes its floor in one action and the corridor beyond the door does not. Choosing
**Default** clears it again, following that material wherever it runs.

Squares revealed later in the same room take the surface already chosen for it.

**Who can do it:** whoever owns the map.

## Correct a square the map got wrong

The map is drawn from walls a GM traced over a picture of walls, so now and then it reads a corner
the way the trace describes it rather than the way the room plainly is. Rather than guess harder, it
takes being told.

Right-click a square and choose **Fix Things**:

- **This is a floor** -- put the square on the map. Where in the square you clicked matters: a square
  a wall cuts through is part floor and part rock, and the point you clicked is taken as the floor
  side.
- **This is not a floor** -- take that one square off. Use this for a corner the map misread.
- **This whole area is not floor** -- take off the entire area walled in with that square. Use this
  to remove a chamber that should not be on the map.

The two removals answer different problems, and the wrong one is destructive: striking a single
square is how a bad corner gets fixed, and flooding from there would take the whole room that corner
opens onto.

Being told beats being worked out. A square struck off stays off however convincingly the sightlines
later argue for it, and walking past will not quietly restore it.

**Who can do it:** whoever owns the map.

## Change how heavily the squares are ruled

Right-click any square and choose **Grid**: **Off**, **Light**, **Medium** or **Dark**.

This one is yours alone. It changes how you see every map, including maps you do not own, and nobody
else's view changes with it.

## What a right-click offers where

On a square that is not on the map, only **Fix Things** appears -- everything else describes floor,
and telling the map that somewhere is floor is only useful where it currently is not.

On an official map that has not been revealed to you, nothing appears, because you cannot see the map
at all.
